# Project 1 — SA NCD Trend Analysis: Diabetes & Hypertension (2012–2022)

Excel-based trend analysis of non-communicable disease burden across four South African provinces over a decade, benchmarked against SA's 2030 National Strategic Plan targets.

---

## Overview

South Africa faces a growing NCD crisis - diabetes and hypertension prevalence have increased consistently across all provinces over the past decade, placing significant pressure on a health system already burdened by HIV and TB. This project analyses that trend, compares provincial performance, and quantifies the gap between current awareness and treatment rates and SA's 2030 NSP targets.

The analysis is relevant to NHI implementation planning, provincial health budget allocation, and public health programme design.

---

## Dataset

- **Source:** Published SA health surveys and International Diabetes Federation (IDF) estimates
- **Period:** 2012 — 2022 (6 time points)
- **Provinces covered:** Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, Western Cape, Eastern Cape
- **Records:** 24 rows across 4 provinces and 6 years
- **Variables:** Diabetes prevalence (%), hypertension prevalence (%), hypertension awareness (%), on treatment (%), controlled (%), population (millions)

## Key Findings

1. **Diabetes prevalence grew by an average of 41% across all four provinces between 2012 and 2022** - a trajectory that, if continued, will place significant pressure on NHI chronic disease financing and drug procurement budgets.

2. **Western Cape carries the highest average diabetes burden (10.2%) yet shows the strongest awareness rates** - suggesting more developed screening and primary care infrastructure compared to other provinces. This highlights the importance of investing in screening capacity, not just treatment.

3. **All four provinces remain approximately 40 percentage points below the 2030 NSP hypertension awareness target of 90%** - indicating that SA is less than halfway to its own policy commitment, with fewer than 8 years remaining to close the gap.
